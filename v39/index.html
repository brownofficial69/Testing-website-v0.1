THE SCIENCE

The SIR model (Kermack and McKendrick, 1927) splits a population into susceptible, infected and recovered. It was built for disease, and it describes worms just as well.

Real networks have hubs. In 2001 Pastor-Satorras and Vespignani showed that on such networks the epidemic threshold nearly vanishes, which is why patching the best-connected machines beats patching at random.
