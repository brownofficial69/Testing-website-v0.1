THE SCIENCE

The birthday paradox. In a room of 23 people there is a better than even chance that two share a birthday, even though there are 365 days. Pairs grow faster than people: k items make about k²/2 pairs.

Why it matters. To find two inputs with the same n-bit hash you need about 2n/2 tries, not 2n. That is why a 160-bit hash gives only 80 bits of collision resistance.

In 2017 researchers published the first practical SHA-1 collision, called SHAttered. It needed roughly 263 computations, cheaper than the 280 the birthday bound predicts, because SHA-1 also has structural weaknesses.

The hash here is a toy 32-bit mixer cut down to n bits, so the experiment finishes in your browser.
